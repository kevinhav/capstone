## 2. The precision gap (the actual problem the project addresses)

Framing arc: the *hardware* layer solved consistency (Ooni/Gozney = temperature control as a product). The *recipe/formulation* layer never did — it's still forum folklore and n=1 blog posts. That gap is what a learned style-space over baker's-percentage data is for.

### Imprecise volume measurement
- King Arthur's own baking blog (nice tie-in — same brand you're already citing above): a "cup" of all-purpose flour weighs **~120g** using their recommended fluff/spoon/level method, but can weigh **up to ~160g** if scooped densely packed from the bag — a ~33% swing in the same nominal "1 cup," from technique alone. ([King Arthur Baking blog](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/10/13/measure-flour))
  - This is a direct, concrete link back to your own data-ingestion plan: it's *why* the RecipeKG/Recipe1M+ ingredient parsing and volume→weight conversion step matters, not just an engineering formality — it's the actual noise source the model has to absorb.
- **Caution**: I found a much more dramatic-sounding stat elsewhere ("NSF-certified lab testing across 47 commercial flours," 51% variance range, "4.3× variance in cake height") on a low-authority SEO site (bakingscalepro.com). The specificity reads like fabricated/AI-generated content, not a real study — I could not verify it and would not cite it. Stick with the King Arthur figure above, which is a primary source from one of your own anchor brands.

### Oven temperature variability
- America's Test Kitchen (via ThermoWorks' blog): home ovens cycle on/off around a thermostat setpoint and periodically deviate "at least a few degrees" continuously during a bake; ovens set to the identical nominal temperature have been measured varying by **as much as 90°F** unit-to-unit. ([ThermoWorks blog](https://blog.thermoworks.com/thermal-secrets-oven-calibration/))
- Implication for the pitch: proofing time, crumb structure, and crust browning are all temperature-sensitive; a recipe tuned on one oven can fail on another from thermostat drift alone — an argument for why the "coefficient tweak" model needs to reason about equipment variance, not just ingredient ratios.

### Altitude & humidity
- High altitude: lower atmospheric pressure causes faster/over-proofing (dough rises faster, can collapse) and lower ambient humidity dries dough during mixing and proofing. ([The Perfect Loaf](https://www.theperfectloaf.com/how-to-bake-sourdough-bread-at-high-altitude/), [Elevation Baking](https://www.elevationbaking.com/high-altitude-baking-adjustments))
- Standard practitioner fix: a flat **+5–10% hydration** bump at altitude — a concrete, quantifiable, testable adjustment coefficient, and a good illustrative example of exactly the kind of "directed coefficient tweak" the project is meant to automate/generalize.
- Day-to-day humidity swings *independent of altitude* can shift effective dough hydration by **3–7%** — described in one source as "enough to transform a well-structured, open-crumb boule into a slack, over-fermented mess or a dry, crumbly brick." (Source: a baking-focused Substack essay, lensandwhisk.substack.com — treat as illustrative/anecdotal, not an authoritative citation, but a good pull-quote for framing.)

### Lack of control / sample size in "homebrewed" recipes
- Forum-and-blog recipe culture (pizzamaking.com, r/Pizza, TheFreshLoaf, personal blogs) means most published recipes are effectively **n=1 anecdotes**: one baker, one oven, one altitude, one flour brand — often not even reproduced twice by the original author.
- No standard practice of reporting flour protein %, water temperature, ambient kitchen humidity, or oven calibration alongside a published "recipe."
- Worth an honest acknowledgment in the proposal itself, not just as a motivator: your training data (RecipeDB/RecipeKG/Recipe1M+) will *also* lack most of these covariates. The argument isn't "clean data now exists" — it's that a model trained across thousands of loosely-controlled recipes can average out individual n=1 noise in a way no single blog post can, and can turn known adjustment heuristics (e.g., altitude → +hydration) into principled, quantified, generalizable recommendations instead of scattered forum folklore.

## 3. Suggested arc (messaging outline, not prose)

1. **Open on the money**: Ooni $200M+ peak revenue, Gozney +62% YoY with $29M+ raised, King Arthur +58% flour sales in 2020 and now the #1 branded flour in the US — the market has voted with its wallet twice: once on ovens, once on ingredients.
2. **Pivot to the gap**: hardware makers (Ooni, Gozney) built entire companies solving *equipment* consistency — their whole pitch is temperature control. Nobody has solved *recipe* consistency. That space is still filled by forum folklore and single-author blog posts.
3. **Name the specific failure modes**, each with a citable number: volume measurement (~33% swing per King Arthur's own data), oven drift (up to 90°F unit-to-unit per ATK), altitude/humidity (3–10% hydration swings), and the structural lack of covariate reporting in homebrewed recipes.
4. **Land the thesis**: a learned, quantitative style-space over baker's-percentage data can absorb this noise at scale and produce principled, generalizable coefficient recommendations — something no single recipe author, forum thread, or blog post can offer.
5. **Optional closing hook**: tie back to the data-sourcing work already done this session — you've already identified and quantified ~3,550+ relevant bread/pizza-dough recipes across RecipeKG, with Recipe1M+ access pending. This isn't a "would be nice" proposal, it's already scoped and data-validated.
